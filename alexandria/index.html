Alexandria's Lost Geography

الجغرافيا المفقودة للإسكندرية

Archival research on the city's heritage sites, read through historical maps and GIS, and the stories behind the buildings that remain.

I. The Question

Every visit home to Alexandria brought news that another building had come down. This project begins from that experience and turns it into a method. If the city cannot keep all of its buildings, it can at least keep a record of where they stood, what they looked like, and who remembers them.

Alexandria's past is layered: Ptolemaic, Roman, Mamluk, Ottoman, cosmopolitan and modern. It is also spread across archives in Alexandria, Cairo, Paris and London. The work here lays historical plans over today's streets and asks three questions of each site. What stood here? What happened to it? What is still standing, and why?

IV. Method

  1. Gather

    Historical plans, cadastral sheets, photographs, guidebooks, press and heritage lists, starting from the collections of the Bibliotheca Alexandrina.

  2. Georeference

    Fix each historical sheet to modern coordinates with ground control points (in QGIS), and record the error for every sheet.

  3. Read across time

    Compare the same block on maps from different decades to see when a building appears, changes or disappears, and set those dates against the written record.

  4. Walk

    Visit each site to record what remains today, and collect the stories of the people who live and work nearby.

Status, as used here

Standing
Survives in recognisable form.
Altered
Survives, but heavily rebuilt, cut back or with its setting changed.
Threatened
At risk of demolition, or partly demolished.
Lost
Demolished or destroyed.
Removed
Survives, but taken away from Alexandria.
