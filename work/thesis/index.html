§ Abstract

This thesis examines how proximity to unresolved conflict shapes strategic non-reconstruction as a heritage practice in Beirut through a comparative discourse analysis of Beit Beirut and the Beirut Port Grain Silos. Using Critical Discourse Analysis and sixteen primary sources (2002–2025), it argues that damaged heritage is deliberately preserved as memory, evidence, and political claim-making rather than neglected ruin. The findings show that temporal proximity shapes different forms of preservation depending on the relationship between communities and unresolved violence. The thesis contributes to heritage and memory studies by introducing proximity as an analytical variable in post-conflict heritage debates.
